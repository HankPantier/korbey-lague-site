## Check Your Refund Status in Seconds

The fastest way to find out where your refund stands is to go straight to the source. The IRS **Where's My Refund?** tool updates once per day — usually overnight — and covers federal returns filed electronically or by mail.

**Check your federal refund:** [IRS Where's My Refund?](https://www.irs.gov/refunds) — available 24/7 on the IRS website or through the IRS2Go mobile app.

**Check your Massachusetts state refund:** [MassTaxConnect Refund Status](https://www.mass.gov/info-details/check-the-status-of-your-tax-refund) — the Commonwealth's official refund tracker, also updated daily.

Most e-filed federal returns appear in the system within 24 hours. Paper returns take up to four weeks to show up. State timelines vary, but Massachusetts typically processes e-filed returns within four to six weeks.

## What You'll Need Before You Check

Both the IRS and Massachusetts tools ask for the same basic information. Have these three items ready before you click:

- **Social Security Number (SSN)** — or your Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN) if that's what you filed with
- **Filing status** — Single, Married Filing Jointly, Married Filing Separately, Head of Household, or Qualifying Surviving Spouse
- **Exact refund amount** — the whole dollar amount shown on your return, not an estimate

The refund amount must match what's on your filed return exactly. If you're unsure, pull up a copy of your 1040 (Line 35a) or your Massachusetts Form 1 (Line 42). If Korbey Lague PLLP prepared your return, your copy is in your client portal.

## Understanding Your Refund Status Message

Once you check, you'll see one of three status messages. Here's what each one actually means:

**Return Received** — The IRS (or Massachusetts DOR) has your return and it's in the processing queue. Nothing is required from you at this stage. This status typically appears within 24 hours of an e-filed submission and can last anywhere from a few days to a few weeks.

**Refund Approved** — Your return cleared review and a refund amount has been confirmed. The IRS has either scheduled a direct deposit date or is preparing a paper check. At this point, you're in the home stretch.

**Refund Sent** — The money is on its way. For direct deposit, funds typically arrive within five business days of this status appearing. Paper checks take up to four weeks from the date the check is mailed.

Massachusetts uses similar language through MassTaxConnect — you may see "Processed," "Refund Issued," or a specific deposit date. If the tracker shows no record of your return at all, it usually means the return hasn't been fully entered yet, not that something is wrong.

## Why Is My Refund Delayed?

Most refunds arrive without a hitch. When they don't, the delay usually comes down to one of these reasons:

**Math errors or missing information.** The IRS corrects simple errors automatically, but the correction adds processing time. If something is missing — a W-2, a 1099, a signature — the return sits until it's resolved.

**Identity verification.** The IRS flags returns that show signs of possible identity theft. If your return is selected, you'll receive a letter (Letter 5071C or similar) asking you to verify your identity online or by phone. Don't ignore it — the refund clock doesn't restart until you respond.

**PATH Act hold.** If you claimed the Earned Income Tax Credit (EITC) or Additional Child Tax Credit (ACTC), federal law requires the IRS to hold your refund until at least mid-February, regardless of how early you filed. This isn't an error — it's a statutory requirement.

**Amended returns (Form 1040-X).** Amended returns are processed manually. The IRS estimates 16 weeks for processing, and the Where's My Refund? tool doesn't track amended returns — use the separate [Where's My Amended Return?](https://www.irs.gov/filing/wheres-my-amended-return) tool instead.

**Debt offsets.** If you owe back taxes, child support, or certain federal debts, the IRS Bureau of the Fiscal Service may reduce your refund automatically. You'll receive a notice explaining the offset.

## When to Call the IRS — and When to Call Us

Waiting is usually the right move if your return was filed correctly and the tracker shows a normal status. The IRS asks taxpayers not to call unless the tool specifically directs you to do so — hold times are long and agents can't see information the tracker doesn't already show you.

**Call the IRS (1-800-829-1040) if:**
- It's been more than 21 days since you e-filed, or more than six weeks since you mailed a paper return, and the tracker shows no update
- You received a notice asking you to call or verify your identity
- The refund amount shown in the tracker is different from what you expected

**Call Korbey Lague PLLP if:**
- You received an IRS or Massachusetts DOR notice and aren't sure how to respond
- Your return may have contained an error that needs an amended filing
- The offset notice references a debt you don't recognize or believe is incorrect
- You want a second set of eyes on a situation before it becomes a bigger problem
