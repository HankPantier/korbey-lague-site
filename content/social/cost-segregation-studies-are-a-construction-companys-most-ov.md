## LinkedIn

A construction company owner who built a $1.2M facility and never commissioned a cost segregation study may have left $90,000–$150,000 in accelerated federal deductions on the table — deductions that would otherwise trickle out over 39 years.

Cost segregation reclassifies building components — specialty electrical, process plumbing, land improvements, storage systems — from 39-year depreciation schedules to 5-, 7-, or 15-year ones. Contractors are stronger candidates than most real estate investors because self-performed work generates the line-item cost records that make reclassification defensible.

Bonus depreciation is phasing down: 40% in 2025, 20% in 2026, then gone under current law. The window to capture maximum benefit is narrowing.

## Instagram

Most contractors assume cost segregation is a real estate investor's tool. It isn't — and that assumption is expensive.

A construction company that built or renovated a $1.2M facility could have $250,000–$400,000 in components eligible for reclassification. At a 37% federal rate, that's $90,000–$150,000 in deductions that would otherwise stretch across nearly four decades. The reason contractors are actually stronger candidates than passive investors: you built the asset, you have the job cost records, and those records are exactly what an engineering study needs to support reclassification.

Bonus depreciation is phasing out. It was 60% in 2024, it's 40% in 2025, and 20% in 2026 before it's scheduled to expire. If your business owns a facility and you've never had this conversation with your CPA, the time to have it is before the rate drops again.
