## LinkedIn

A single-member LLC generating $200,000 in net profit pays self-employment tax on every dollar of it — roughly $28,000 a year. The same business structured as an S-corp, with a $90,000 reasonable salary and $110,000 in distributions, cuts that bill to around $13,000. That's $15,000 back in the business, annually, from a structural decision most Massachusetts service owners made once at formation and never revisited.

The break-even for an S-corp election sits around $60,000 in net profit. Below that, payroll administration costs eat the savings. Above it, the math shifts quickly — and keeps shifting as revenue grows.

## Instagram

Most Massachusetts service business owners chose their entity structure once — probably at startup — and haven't looked at it since. That single decision, unexamined, can cost five figures a year.

Here's the scenario we see constantly: a consultant or agency owner running $200,000 in net profit through a single-member LLC. Every dollar hits their Schedule SE. Self-employment tax alone runs about $28,000. Elect S-corp status, set a defensible $90,000 salary, and take the remaining $110,000 as a distribution — now that tax bill drops to roughly $13,000. Same profit. Different structure. $15,000 difference.

The S-corp election doesn't always win. Below about $60,000 in net profit, payroll administration costs offset the savings. Massachusetts also layers on its own entity-level fees and corporate excise rules that national guides tend to ignore. The right answer depends on your profit level, ownership structure, and where you are in the business lifecycle.
